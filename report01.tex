\section{Project Objective}

The objective of the project is to support the operation of the ECP5 FPGA device by providing a complete development platform for it. Our current objective is to identify the requirements of the ECP5 module and to design a system-level architecture of what needs to be achieved and how. The project will be developed in a modular manner, with each subsystem being designed independently before being integrated into a complete system.

\section{System Architecture Overview}

To support the ECP5 FPGA effectively, the system has been partitioned into six core hardware subsystems:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5cm]
    \item Subsystem 1: Power Subsystem
    \item Subsystem 2: Configuration and Programming Interface
    \item Subsystem 3: PCB Layout and Mechanical Constraints
    \item Subsystem 4: Clocking and Timing Network
    \item Subsystem 5: User I/O and Peripheral Interfaces
    \item Subsystem 6: Debugging and Telemetry Infrastructure
\end{enumerate}

The exact implementation of these subsystems will depend on the selected ECP5 device/package, the required peripherals, the available board area, and other project requirements.

The reference architecture studied for initial baseline requirements is the Lattice ECP5-5G Evaluation Board (\texttt{LFE5UM5G-85F-8BG381}), adapted to meet the cost, layer-count, and peripheral constraints of this project. The purpose of studying the evaluation board is not to reproduce the design directly, but to use it as a practical reference for identifying the types of supporting circuits and power domains that may be required. The final board architecture will be adapted according to the requirements and constraints of the present project.

\section{Subsystem Progress Reports}

\subsection{Subsystem 1: Power Subsystem}
\noindent\textbf{Lead:} Pranjal Pandey (SC25B128)

\subsubsection{Overview and Design Strategy}
The power subsystem must provide clean, stable rails across several distinct domains. Rather than choosing discrete regulators immediately, the current method of identifies all power consumption endpoints from device datasheets and works backwards toward source selection, regulator topologies, and filtering networks. At this stage the purpose of the investigation is not to immediately select regulator components, but to identify the general power endpoints and work backwards towards the architecture of the power subsystem.

From the preliminary investigation of the ECP5 requirements and the reference evaluation board, the required electrical domains are categorized in Table~\ref{tab:power_domains}.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{lp{5.5cm}p{6cm}}
\toprule
\textbf{Rail Name} & \textbf{Target Domain} & \textbf{Nominal Voltage \& Specifications} \\
\midrule
$V_{\text{CC}}$        & FPGA Digital Core Logic        & 1.1\,V (ECP5) / 1.2\,V (ECP5-5G) \\
$V_{\text{CCAUX}}$     & Auxiliary / Logic Buffers      & 2.5\,V ($\pm 5\%$, ramp $\le 30\,\text{mV}/\mu\text{s}$) \\
$V_{\text{CCIO[0..8]}}$& I/O Bank Drivers (Banks 0--8)  & Configurable: 1.2\,V, 1.5\,V, 1.8\,V, 2.5\,V, 3.3\,V \\
$V_{\text{CCA}}$       & SerDes Analog Core (if used)   & 1.1\,V (ECP5UM) / 1.2\,V (ECP5-5G) \\
$V_{\text{CCHTX}}$     & SerDes TX Buffers (if used)    & 1.1\,V (ECP5UM) / 1.2\,V (ECP5-5G) \\
$V_{\text{PERIPH}}$    & External Oscillators / Flash   & 3.3\,V / 2.5\,V depending on component specs \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Preliminary power-domain specification and allocation}
\label{tab:power_domains}
\end{table}

The exact number of $V_{\text{CCIO}}$ rails required will depend on the selected FPGA package and the allocation of I/O banks to different interfaces, making FPGA package selection and I/O planning an important dependency for the final power architecture. Similarly, the external peripheral rail ($V_{\text{PERIPH}}$) will depend on the specific peripherals chosen and will have to wait until peripheral selection is complete.

\subsubsection{Power Distribution Architecture}
The planned power-delivery pipeline follows a hierarchical tree:
\begin{center}
External DC Input (e.g., 5\,V USB-C / 12\,V Jack)\\
$\downarrow$\\
Primary Switching Step-Down Converters (Buck: 3.3\,V, 2.5\,V, 1.2\,V Core)\\
$\downarrow$\\
Secondary Linear Regulation / LDOs (Analog Isolation for SerDes/PLLs)\\
$\downarrow$\\
Decoupling Networks \& Ferrite Beads $\rightarrow$ FPGA BGA Balls
\end{center}

For each required rail, the following parameters will be determined:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5cm]
    \item Required voltage
    \item Estimated current requirement
    \item Expected peak/transient requirement
    \item Source/input voltage
    \item Regulator topology
    \item Decoupling requirements
    \item Monitoring and test points
\end{enumerate}

Once these requirements are established, suitable regulator and supporting components can be selected and incorporated into the BOM.

\subsubsection{Next Steps for the Power Subsystem}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1cm]
    \item Perform static and dynamic current estimations using the Lattice Diamond Power Calculator.
    \item Establish power sequencing requirements ($V_{\text{CCIO}}$ ramp relative to $V_{\text{CC}}$ and $V_{\text{CCAUX}}$ to ensure clean Power-On Reset).
    \item Select regulator ICs based on thermal dissipation, PCB area, and efficiency targets.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}

The first stage of the project has been focused on converting the general requirement of developing an ECP5-based FPGA board into a set of identifiable engineering problems. The power subsystem has been selected as the initial area of detailed work: the current approach is to identify all required power endpoints, determine their electrical requirements, and subsequently work backwards toward regulator selection and a complete power architecture.

Further work will integrate this subsystem with FPGA configuration, clocking, I/O, debugging, and PCB layout to produce the complete development-board design.
